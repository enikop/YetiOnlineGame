Blue Yeti
Multiplayer didactic
game for improper integrals and infinite sums
Gameplay
Blue Yeti can be played by 4 players at a time. In each round, the players take their turns sequentially. A turn consists of two phases:
  • Drawing
    The drawing phase, with an allocated timer of {{drawingDuration}} seconds, refers to picking a card from the hand of the next player in line by clicking on it. The newly drawn card is placed randomly into the current player's hand but is highlighted by a dark border section.
  • Pairing
    In the pairing phase, the current player forms valid pairs of the infinite sums/improper integrals in their hand. A valid pair is made up of a divergent sum/integral and its divergent minorant or a convergent sum/integral and its convergent majorant. The inequality between the members of a pair should always be strict. Next, the current player places the pairs onto the correct field (convergent or divergent) of the pairing area. If no more valid pairs are found, the End turn button can be clicked, marking the start of the following drawing phase.
If the timers ever run out, drawing is done randomly, while the pairing phase simply ends. The objective of the players is to get rid of all their cards. Once they succeed to do so, they are out of the game. In the end, the last player holding the pairless Blue Yeti card is deemed the loser of the game.
Drawing
Drawing in the game is implemented in a similar way to manual card games, meaning that the players can organise their hands freely, and the current player picking e. g. the second card from the left in the UI means the card that has been put in the second position by the next player in line will be drawn. This is why continuous rearranging of one's hand is advised, even though newly drawn cards are put in random positions.
Pairing
In the game Blue Yeti, pairing is done based on the direct comparison test of infinite series and improper integrals. These two topics are interconnected through the integral test of infinte series.
Integral test
Consider an integer N and a non-negative function f defined on the unbounded interval [N, ∞), on which it is monotone decreasing. Then the infinite series converges to a real number if and only if the improper integral converges.
Therefore, it is sufficient to remember the convergence tests applicable to infinite series to deduce the convergence property of the corresponding improper integrals accordingly.
