Blue Yeti
Multiplayer didactic
game for improper integrals and infinite sums
Gameplay
Blue Yeti can be played by 4 players at a time. In each round, the players take their turns sequentially. A turn consists of two phases:
  • Drawing
    The drawing phase, with an allocated timer of {{drawingDuration}} seconds, refers to picking a card from the hand of the next player in line by clicking on it. The newly drawn card is placed randomly into the current player's hand but is highlighted by a dark border section.
  • Pairing
    In the pairing phase, the current player forms valid pairs of the infinite sums/improper integrals in their hand. A valid pair is made up of a divergent sum/integral and its divergent minorant or a convergent sum/integral and its convergent majorant. The inequality between the members of a pair should always be strict. Next, the current player places the pairs onto the correct field (convergent or divergent) of the pairing area. Any pairs put onto the central card slots are submitted automatically. If a faulty pairing is made, the player's turn ends. The previous correct pairings in a game, made by all the players, can be seen in the history sidebar on the left of the screen, that can be opened by clicking the clock symbol in the top left corner. If no more valid pairs are found, the End turn button can be clicked, marking the start of the following drawing phase.
If the timers ever run out, drawing is done randomly, while the pairing phase simply ends. The objective of the players is to get rid of all their cards. Once they succeed to do so, they are out of the game. In the end, the last player holding the pairless Blue Yeti card is deemed the loser of the game and the list of results containing the number of mistakes made by players in the game is displayed.
Drawing
Drawing in the game is implemented in a similar way to manual card games, meaning that the players can organise their hands freely, and the current player picking e. g. the second card from the left in the UI means the card that has been put in the second position by the next player in line will be drawn. This is why continuous rearranging of one's hand is advised, even though newly drawn cards are put in random positions.

Bots
If a player quits a game that has already started, a bot operated by the backend server takes their place. Bots are capable of drawing cards randomly and making one correct pairing per round if their hands allow. Therefore, they can win or lose a game anytime, just like regular humans. However, the time limits for bot moves are set much lower, allocating 1-1 seconds for both the drawing and the pairing phase to promote a smooth gameplay experience and avoid unnecessary delays. The pairing process of bots is very fast, so it is recommended to use the history sidebar to observe their pairings.
When a bot comes into play, all other players are notified in a message that appears at the top of their screen. The summary of a game's results also specifies whether a given player was a bot or a human.
For beginners
Beginners can benefit from the Practice feature of Blue Yeti, referring to a simple quiz where players have to guess the convergence property of randomly chosen infinite series or improper integrals. The correct answers are revealed immediately. Each correct guess earns a point, while each faulty response makes a short explanation related to the subtype of the given sum or integral appear. At the end of the quiz, a summary is displayed containing the total number of correct and incorrect guesses, as well as the solution time.
In addition, a special game mode of Blue Yeti is available directly from the main menu named assisted mode. In this mode, a player is kept up to date about the number of possible pairs in their hand via a counter next to their username. They can also access particular convergence rules applicable to a given sum or integral by hovering over the card featuring said sum/integral.
